# Teaching strategy and product priorities

1 October 2026. This is the recommended learning design, not a claim that all stages are shipped or that a particular study duration guarantees marks.

## Recommended learning loop

Try a small diagnostic before teaching. Repair the specific error with selected-option contrast. Ask for recall with changed wording. Later mix the concept with another reviewed topic. Recheck after a meaningful delay. Prioritize successful transfer and delayed retention over total questions clicked.

For history, connect each concept to **problem → proposal → outcome**, with dates and actors attached to the same short timeline. Keep proposal, agreement, legislation and implementation dates distinct. A timeline is an orientation aid, not a substitute for precise provisions.

Use authentic PYQs as the anchor once their paper and final key are authenticated. Attach separately labelled generated variants: reverse the cue, compare provisions, reorder dates, judge statements, and notice negative wording. Do not copy an answer-changing variant of a source question and retain its PYQ label. Preserve some untouched authenticated questions as held-out progress checks.

## What makes the teaching useful

- Ask both directions: Central University proposal → Six-Point Formula; old residence-based employment eligibility → Mulki Rules. Avoid teaching that one option is always correct.
- Require an explanation or recall before showing MCQ options occasionally. Treat self-reported recall separately from objectively scored answers.
- Offer two or three possible mistake reasons after an error, with an optional learner response. Do not assume why someone selected an option; confidence and timing are imperfect signals.
- Use small, targeted lessons after errors. Offer optional fuller background; do not force an essay after every independently correct answer.
- On exam approach, mix topics and question forms, reduce hints, and practise authentic timing. Do not apply speed pressure before basic understanding.
- Preserve a syllabus coverage floor: frequently seen PYQs guide priorities but do not prove what will appear next. Do not fabricate predicted probabilities from a small sample.
- Track unseen-question performance, delayed no-hint recall, model weaknesses and repeated confusion pairs separately. Avoid one inflated mastery percentage.

## Suggested daily routine

Start with a 40-minute budget: 8 minutes due recall, 12 minutes one small concept, 12 minutes varied practice, 8 minutes explanation from memory and review planning. This split is a product recommendation, not an experimentally established optimum. Adapt to time available, exam date and fatigue. Missed days should produce a manageable catch-up queue rather than guilt or an endless backlog.

Spacing can start with a next-session/day check and extend after successful independent recall; shorten after failure. Exact intervals are heuristics to be evaluated, not a universal scientifically optimal sequence. A same-session correct retry is evidence of repair, not long-term memory.

## Evidence and limits

- Karpicke & Roediger (2008), *The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning*: repeated retrieval improved delayed vocabulary recall in their experiments. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408).
- Cepeda et al. (2006), *Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks*: reviewed spacing experiments; useful spacing depends on the intended retention interval. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/).
- IES (2007), *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning*: recommends spacing, retrieval quizzes and explanatory questions, with differing evidence strengths. [Practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1).

These support the learning principles. They do not establish this app's effectiveness for Telangana Police examinations. Test actual unseen-question accuracy and delayed retention with aspirants before claiming gains.

## Build order

1. **Current delivery:** immediate simple-English teaching for every existing option, selected/correct comparison, observation cues, conditional question rules, saved mistake explanations; versioned corpus and explicit publication review.
2. **Next content gate:** authenticate official SI/Constable papers and final keys; source each fact; widen reviewed coverage across the three syllabus phases.
3. **Adaptive learning gate:** per-concept diagnostics, exposure-aware variants, learner-confirmed mistake reasons, optional recall-before-options, interleaving across reviewed families and manageable catch-up.
4. **Exam-readiness gate:** held-out PYQs, full-paper timing, independent delayed assessment and learner evaluation.
5. **Production operations gate:** authentication, access control, database backups/restore, monitoring, privacy controls and supported deployment.

The most valuable next investment is reviewed content breadth. An unrestricted chatbot or unlimited automatic question generation would not solve the current coverage gap.
